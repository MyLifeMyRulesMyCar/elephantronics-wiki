This beginner project builds a four-wheel robot car that drives forward until an
ultrasonic sensor detects an obstacle. It then stops, backs up, scans left and
right with a servo-mounted sensor, and turns toward the side with more space.

The build has four stages: chassis assembly, electronics placement, wiring, and
testing with calibration. The wiring section is the most detailed part; check
every connection before applying power.

<Callout variant="warning" title="Draft build">
  The instructions follow Elephantronics' published tutorial, but the robot has
  not been built or measured for this wiki. The source code is only available as
  two external Google Drive folders, and some source photos were not available.
  Treat this page as a draft and verify the wiring against your own hardware.
</Callout>

## Safety before you start

- Disconnect the battery before changing any wiring.
- A 3 × 18650 pack is about 11.1 V nominal. Check the pack, switch, and L298N
  input rating before connecting them.
- Keep a **common ground** between the battery, L298N, Arduino, and breadboard.
- Do not power the servo from the Arduino 5 V pin while the motors are running.
- Secure the robot before the first motor test; it can drive off a table.

## Components

| Component | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Arduino Uno | Runs the control program |
| L298N motor driver module | Controls motor direction and speed |
| 4 × DC gear motors + wheels | Drive the four wheels |
| HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor + bracket | Measures distance to an obstacle |
| SG90 180° servo | Rotates the sensor to scan left and right |
| 3 × 18650 battery holder + batteries | Main power supply, about 11.1 V nominal |
| Mini breadboard | 5 V and ground distribution |
| On/off switch | Switches the battery line |
| Chassis plates, 6 copper spacers, screws, nuts, motor wires | Mechanical structure and fasteners |

No shop links or prices are included here; source the parts from a supplier you
trust and check the voltage/current ratings.

## Chassis assembly

<Steps>
1. Remove the protective film from both acrylic chassis plates and prepare the screws, nuts, and spacers.
2. Solder one red and one black wire to each DC gear motor. Red is positive; black is negative. Check that the joints are firm.
3. Align the motors with the mounting slots and fix them with screws and nuts. A loose motor makes the robot vibrate or track poorly.
4. Push the wheels onto the motor shafts until they are fully seated.
5. Install the six copper spacers, place the upper chassis plate on top, and tighten the screws.
</Steps>

## Electronics placement

Mount the SG90 servo at the front of the chassis. Attach the ultrasonic bracket
to the servo horn, then mount the HC-SR04 on the bracket so it can scan left and
right. Place the remaining parts in this order:

1. Mini breadboard, fixed with hot glue or double-sided tape
2. Arduino Uno
3. L298N motor driver
4. On/off switch
5. 18650 battery holder

## Wiring

### DC motors to the L298N

| Motor side | Wire colour | L298N terminal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Right side, both motors | Red (positive) | OUT1 |
| Right side, both motors | Black (negative) | OUT2 |
| Left side, both motors | Black (negative) | OUT3 |
| Left side, both motors | Red (positive) | OUT4 |

<Callout variant="note" title="The left side is intentionally reversed">
  The black/red order on the left side is opposite to the right side. That makes
  both sides rotate the same way when the robot moves forward. If the robot spins
  in place during testing, swap the two motor wires on one side.
</Callout>

<Callout variant="caution" title="Verify the Arduino power path">
  The source connects the L298N 5 V output to the Arduino **VIN** pin. On a classic
  Arduino Uno, VIN expects a higher input for the onboard regulator, so this
  connection may not power the board reliably. Some builds feed the Uno through
  the 5 V pin instead, but back-powering through USB or the 5 V pin has its own
  risks. Check the regulator on your boards and measure the 5 V rail before
  connecting the Arduino.
</Callout>

## Testing and calibration

### 1. Motor movement test

Run the motor test **before** the obstacle-avoidance program. The robot should
perform this sequence:

1. Forward
2. Backward
3. Turn right
4. Turn left

Download the sketch from Elephantronics' official Google Drive folder:

- [Motor Test Code](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1gxc3b70aacLbaBtZTAxml7kWeQfcernX?usp=sharing) — opens Google Drive.

Select the correct Arduino board and port, upload the sketch, insert the
batteries, and watch the sequence. If a movement is wrong, swap the two motor
wires on the affected side at the L298N terminals.

<Callout variant="note" title="External code">
  The sketch is hosted outside this wiki. Review it before uploading and confirm
  the pin numbers match the wiring on your robot.
</Callout>

### 2. Obstacle-avoidance code

After the motor test passes, upload the main program:

- [Obstacle Avoiding Code](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1vQ37JGU6ZoZTjKv79yUHecKnehNF55rR?usp=sharing) — opens Google Drive.

In the main loop, the robot measures the distance ahead:

- **No obstacle within 25 cm:** drive forward.
- **Obstacle detected:** stop, reverse slightly, scan right and left with the
  servo, compare the two distances, and turn toward the side with more space.

The 25 cm threshold and the scan angles are examples from the source. Tune them
for your robot's size, speed, and surface.

### 3. Calibration

During the source test, the robot turned almost 180° at every obstacle. The fix
was to reduce the delay in `turnRight()` and `turnLeft()` from **900 ms to
400 ms**. A shorter delay produces a shorter turn.

- Re-upload after changing the delay.
- Tune in steps of about 100 ms until a turn is roughly 90°.
- Carpet and smooth floors may need different values.
- If the robot still over-rotates, reduce the delay further; if it under-rotates,
  increase it slightly.

## Troubleshooting

| Issue | Check |
| --- | --- |
| Robot spins in place | Swap the two motor wires on one side |
| No movement | Battery charge, switch, L298N power, common ground |
| One side does not move | Motor wiring, L298N output, ENA/ENB signal |
| Servo jitters or the Arduino resets | Servo current draw, shared ground, 5 V rail |
| Distance readings are erratic | TRIG/ECHO pins, sensor supply, angle, and reflective surfaces |
| Robot turns too far | Reduce the turn delay |
| Robot does not turn enough | Increase the turn delay slightly |
| Motors run but the sensor does not | D2/D3 wiring and the sketch's pin definitions |
